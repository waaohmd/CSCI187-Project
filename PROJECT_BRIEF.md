# Campus Community Marketplace — Project Brief

**Status:** Canonical project reference for future requests  
**Saved:** 2026-10-19  
**Instruction:** Consult this brief before responding to or acting on every future request about this project. Do not implement features unless the user explicitly asks.

## Vision

Build a student-powered campus marketplace for students living in dorms and nearby apartments. The platform lets students buy, sell, move, and deliver furniture, clothing, appliances, electronics, and other used items. It should create a seamless campus circular economy in which students save money, earn money, and give unwanted items a second life.

## Target users and access

The primary users are college students who:

- Need affordable items.
- Want to sell items they no longer need.
- Want flexible, local ways to earn money.
- Need help transporting items into or out of dorms and nearby apartments.

The project expects direct user access through campus clubs, student organizations, residence halls, university events, and partnerships with schools.

## Problem

Students frequently throw away or leave behind useful items when moving, graduating, or changing apartments because finding buyers and transporting bulky items is inconvenient. At the same time, other students need affordable furniture and household items but lack an easy way to discover and transport those items locally.

## Core solution

Connect buyers and sellers with student movers who can pick up and deliver purchases, often on the same day. A student may participate as a buyer, seller, paid mover, or any combination of these roles. The platform should support marketplace transactions, local delivery and lifting help, quick campus gigs, communication, scheduling, reservations, and secure in-app payments.

## Required user capabilities

Users should be able to:

- Buy and sell used items to other students.
- Receive money efficiently from transactions with other students.
- Pay securely through the app for both items and moving help.
- Have items delivered into or out of a dorm.
- Hire fellow students to lift and move heavy furniture, including carrying it up staircases.
- Choose specific pickup and help time slots that all involved parties mutually agree upon.
- Post quick side gigs, such as hauling or driving, with cash offers for other students.
- Browse and accept quick, low-effort campus tasks to earn extra money on their own schedule.
- Sort available delivery jobs by estimated completion time.
- Maintain contact between buyers and sellers through the app.
- Lock in a purchase with a reservation deposit to reduce buyer or seller no-shows.
- Reserve used items to inspect them in person before paying.
- Meet a seller to see an item before buying.
- Filter marketplace items by verified condition ratings.
- View suggested selling prices based on an item's condition.
- Book doorstep pickup for unwanted items the user wants to donate.
- View an interactive map of campus donation bins and student pop-up sales.

## Primary scenario: Hue's move-out sale

Hue is moving out of his apartment and no longer needs a mini-fridge, desk, and chair. Instead of throwing them away or struggling to sell them through Facebook Marketplace, he lists them on the campus marketplace. Other students browse his listings, view prices and photos, and message him in the app. A student buys the mini-fridge and selects delivery. A student delivery worker picks it up from Hue and brings it to the buyer the same day. Hue earns money from the sale, the buyer receives the item directly at the dorm, and the student deliverer earns money for completing the delivery.

## Additional scenarios

- A time-constrained student completes fast deliveries for other students.
- A buyer meets a seller and inspects an item before buying it.
- A shopper filters listings using verified condition ratings.
- A seller checks a suggested price based on an item's condition.
- A student schedules doorstep pickup to donate unwanted items.
- A buyer reserves an item for an in-person inspection before paying.
- A user explores an interactive campus map showing donation bins and student pop-up sales.

## Product principles inferred from the brief

- **Campus-local:** Discovery, fulfillment, and earning opportunities center on a student's nearby campus community.
- **Multi-role participation:** The same student can buy, sell, deliver, move, or complete gigs.
- **Convenience:** Same-day delivery, agreed time slots, dorm pickup/drop-off, and in-app communication reduce friction.
- **Trust and commitment:** Verified condition ratings, inspection reservations, deposits, and secure payment reduce uncertainty and flaking.
- **Flexible earning:** Students can choose deliveries or small tasks that fit their schedules and sort work by expected duration.
- **Circularity:** Resale and donation keep usable goods out of the waste stream.
